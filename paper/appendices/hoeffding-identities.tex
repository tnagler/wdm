\section{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:weighted-hoeffding-ranks}}
\label{app:hoeffding-identities}

We prove the three rank identities behind
Proposition~\ref{prop:weighted-hoeffding-ranks} by removing diagonals from
unrestricted products of ranks. Fix the anchor observation $i$. Because all
ranks use strict inequalities, any summand in which a comparison index equals
$i$ vanishes, so it remains only to make the comparison indices mutually
distinct.

For the term of order three, expanding the square of $q_i^{(1)}$ and removing
its diagonal gives
\begin{align*}
  \sum_{j\neq k}
  p_jp_k I_{j,i}^{XY}I_{k,i}^{XY}
  &= \left(q_i^{(1)}\right)^2-q_i^{(2)}.
\end{align*}

For the term of order four, inclusion--exclusion over the three equalities
$j=k$, $j=l$, and $k=l$ gives
\begin{align*}
  &\sum_{j,k,l}^{\neq}
  p_jp_kp_l I_{j,i}^{XY}I_{k,i}^X I_{l,i}^Y\\
  &\qquad=
  q_i^{(1)}r_i^{(1)}s_i^{(1)}
  -q_i^{(2)}
   \left(q_i^{(1)}+r_i^{(1)}+s_i^{(1)}\right)
  +2q_i^{(3)}.
\end{align*}
The three pairwise diagonals are subtracted once each; any two of them
intersect in the triple diagonal $j=k=l$, which is therefore added back
twice.

The term of order five has two pairs of comparison indices. First require
distinctness within each pair:
\begin{align*}
  &\sum_{\substack{j\neq k\\l\neq m}}
  p_jp_kp_lp_m I_{j,i}^X I_{k,i}^X I_{l,i}^Y I_{m,i}^Y\\
  &\qquad=
  \left\{\left(r_i^{(1)}\right)^2-r_i^{(2)}\right\}
  \left\{\left(s_i^{(1)}\right)^2-s_i^{(2)}\right\}.
\end{align*}
There are four possible cross-collisions, one for each pairing of an $X$
index with a $Y$ index. The contribution from one specified cross-collision,
after requiring the remaining three indices to be distinct, is
\begin{align*}
  &\sum_{j,k,m}^{\neq}
  p_j^2p_kp_m I_{j,i}^{XY}I_{k,i}^X I_{m,i}^Y\\
  &\qquad=
  \left(r_i^{(1)}s_i^{(1)}-q_i^{(2)}\right)q_i^{(2)}
  -q_i^{(3)}\left(r_i^{(1)}+s_i^{(1)}\right)
  +2q_i^{(4)}.
\end{align*}
If two cross-collisions occur, they must form one of the two matchings between
the two $X$ and two $Y$ indices. The contribution from either matching is
\begin{align*}
  \sum_{j\neq k}p_j^2p_k^2 I_{j,i}^{XY}I_{k,i}^{XY}
  &= \left(q_i^{(2)}\right)^2-q_i^{(4)}.
\end{align*}
Consequently, the fully distinct sum is the within-pair-distinct sum minus
four single-cross-collision sums and minus two double-matching sums. This is
the expression in brackets defining $A_3$.

Multiplication by $p_i$, summation over the anchor $i$, and division by the
ordered distinct-tuple masses $k!e_k(p_1,\ldots,p_n)$ turn the three
identities into the claimed representation of $\widehat D_w$.
