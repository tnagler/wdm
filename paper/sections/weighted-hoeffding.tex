\section{Weighted Hoeffding's \texorpdfstring{$D$}{D}}
\label{sec:weighted-hoeffding}

Hoeffding's $D$ is the first of the two coefficients on which this report
concentrates. We define a weighted estimator through sums over distinct
observations, show that these sums can be expressed through weighted ranks
(Proposition~\ref{prop:weighted-hoeffding-ranks}), and derive an exact
$O(n\log n)$ algorithm from this representation. Throughout this section the
marginal distributions are assumed continuous, so ties occur with probability
zero.

\subsection{Population coefficient and distinct-index estimator}

Let $H$ be the joint distribution function of $(X,Y)$ with marginals $F_X$
and $F_Y$. The normalized version of Hoeffding's coefficient used here is
\begin{align*}
    D
    &= 30\int
       \left\{H(x,y)-F_X(x)F_Y(y)\right\}^2\,\mathrm{d}H(x,y).
\end{align*}
It is zero under independence, and the factor $30$ gives value one under a
strictly monotone functional relation. Hoeffding's original test is based on
an unbiased rank estimator of $D$ \citep{hoeffding1948}.

Expanding the square shows that $D$ is a combination of expectations of orders
three, four, and five. Let $(X^{(j)},Y^{(j)})$, $j=0,\ldots,4$, be independent
copies of $(X,Y)$, and set $I_j^X = \mathbf{1}(X^{(j)}<X^{(0)})$,
$I_j^Y = \mathbf{1}(Y^{(j)}<Y^{(0)})$, and $I_j^{XY}=I_j^XI_j^Y$. Then
\begin{align*}
    \frac{D}{30}
    &= \theta_3-2\theta_4+\theta_5,
    \qquad
    \theta_3
    = \mathbb{E}\bigl[I_1^{XY}I_2^{XY}\bigr],
    \quad
    \theta_4
    = \mathbb{E}\bigl[I_1^{XY}I_2^X I_3^Y\bigr],
    \quad
    \theta_5
    = \mathbb{E}\bigl[I_1^X I_2^X I_3^Y I_4^Y\bigr].
\end{align*}
Each $\theta_k$ is the expectation of a function of $k$ independent copies.
The natural weighted estimator therefore averages over $k$-tuples of distinct
observations, each weighted by the product of its masses. For observations $i$
and $j$, write $I_{j,i}^X = \mathbf{1}(X_j<X_i)$,
$I_{j,i}^Y = \mathbf{1}(Y_j<Y_i)$, and $I_{j,i}^{XY} = I_{j,i}^XI_{j,i}^Y$,
and let $\sum^{\neq}$ denote a sum over pairwise distinct indices. Define
\begin{align*}
    \widehat\theta_{3,w}
    &= \frac{
         \displaystyle
         \sum_{i,j,k}^{\neq}p_ip_jp_k I_{j,i}^{XY}I_{k,i}^{XY}
       }{3!e_3(p_1,\ldots,p_n)},\\
    \widehat\theta_{4,w}
    &= \frac{
         \displaystyle
         \sum_{i,j,k,l}^{\neq}
         p_ip_jp_kp_l I_{j,i}^{XY}I_{k,i}^X I_{l,i}^Y
       }{4!e_4(p_1,\ldots,p_n)},\\
    \widehat\theta_{5,w}
    &= \frac{
         \displaystyle
         \sum_{i,j,k,l,m}^{\neq}
         p_ip_jp_kp_lp_m I_{j,i}^X I_{k,i}^X I_{l,i}^Y I_{m,i}^Y
       }{5!e_5(p_1,\ldots,p_n)}.
\end{align*}
The denominator $k!e_k(p_1,\ldots,p_n)$ is the total mass of ordered distinct
$k$-tuples, so each $\widehat\theta_{k,w}$ is a weighted average. The weighted
Hoeffding estimator is
\begin{align*}
    \widehat D_w
    &= 30\bigl(
         \widehat\theta_{3,w}
         -2\widehat\theta_{4,w}
         +\widehat\theta_{5,w}
       \bigr),
\end{align*}
which requires at least five observations with positive mass.

Two properties follow directly from the construction. If the observations are
independent and identically distributed and the weights are deterministic or
independent of the observations, every distinct tuple has expectation
$\theta_k$, so $\widehat\theta_{k,w}$ is unbiased for $\theta_k$ and
$\widehat D_w$ is unbiased for $D$. Uniform weights give
$k!e_k(1/n,\ldots,1/n) = n(n-1)\cdots(n-k+1)/n^k$ and recover Hoeffding's
ordinary estimator. Observation-dependent weights instead define a reweighted
target and require the sampling assumptions discussed in
Section~\ref{subsec:test-framework}.

\subsection{Rank representation}

Direct evaluation of the distinct-index sums would cost up to $O(n^5)$
operations. The following proposition collapses them to sums over one anchor
index of products of weighted ranks. For $a=1,\ldots,4$, define
\begin{align*}
    r_i^{(a)}
    &= \sum_{j=1}^n p_j^a I_{j,i}^X,
    &
    s_i^{(a)}
    &= \sum_{j=1}^n p_j^a I_{j,i}^Y,
    &
    q_i^{(a)}
    &= \sum_{j=1}^n p_j^a I_{j,i}^{XY}.
\end{align*}
Thus $r_i^{(1)}$ and $s_i^{(1)}$ are the strict weighted marginal ranks and
$q_i^{(1)} = H_{i,w}^{-}$ is the strict weighted bivariate rank of
Section~\ref{subsec:weighted-ranks}; for $a\geq2$ the same ranks are computed
with the $a$th powers of the masses.

\begin{proposition}[Rank representation]
\label{prop:weighted-hoeffding-ranks}
Let at least five case masses be positive. Define
\begin{align*}
  A_1
  &= \sum_{i=1}^n p_i
     \left\{\left(q_i^{(1)}\right)^2-q_i^{(2)}\right\},\\
  A_2
  &= \sum_{i=1}^n p_i
     \left\{
       r_i^{(1)}s_i^{(1)}q_i^{(1)}
       -q_i^{(2)}\left(r_i^{(1)}+s_i^{(1)}+q_i^{(1)}\right)
       +2q_i^{(3)}
     \right\},\\
  A_3
  &= \sum_{i=1}^n p_i
     \Bigl[
       \left\{\left(r_i^{(1)}\right)^2-r_i^{(2)}\right\}
       \left\{\left(s_i^{(1)}\right)^2-s_i^{(2)}\right\}\\
  &\qquad
       -4\left\{
         \left(r_i^{(1)}s_i^{(1)}-q_i^{(2)}\right)q_i^{(2)}
         -q_i^{(3)}\left(r_i^{(1)}+s_i^{(1)}\right)
         +2q_i^{(4)}
       \right\}\\
  &\qquad
       -2\left\{\left(q_i^{(2)}\right)^2-q_i^{(4)}\right\}
     \Bigr].
\end{align*}
Then the distinct-tuple estimator satisfies
\begin{align*}
  \widehat D_w
  &= 30\left\{
       \frac{A_1}{3!e_3(p_1,\ldots,p_n)}
       -\frac{2A_2}{4!e_4(p_1,\ldots,p_n)}
       +\frac{A_3}{5!e_5(p_1,\ldots,p_n)}
     \right\}.
\end{align*}
\end{proposition}

The powers of the masses are diagonal corrections, not an additional weighting
scheme. In $A_1$, the identity
\begin{align*}
    \left(q_i^{(1)}\right)^2-q_i^{(2)}
    &= \sum_{j\neq k}p_jp_k I_{j,i}^{XY}I_{k,i}^{XY}
\end{align*}
removes the terms with $j=k$ from the square of the bivariate rank;
multiplication by the anchor mass $p_i$ and summation over $i$ gives the
order-three sum. In $A_2$, the unrestricted product
$q_i^{(1)}r_i^{(1)}s_i^{(1)}$ allows its three comparison indices to
coincide, and inclusion--exclusion over the three pairwise equalities removes
the diagonals. In $A_3$, the first product makes the two $X$ indices distinct
and the two $Y$ indices distinct. Four cross-collisions between an $X$ index
and a $Y$ index remain: the term multiplied by four is the mass of
configurations with one specified cross-collision, and the term multiplied by
two removes the two matchings with two cross-collisions. Comparison indices
equal to the anchor $i$ vanish automatically because all ranks use strict
inequalities. Appendix~\ref{app:hoeffding-identities} gives the fully indexed
calculation.

\subsection{Fast algorithm}
\label{subsec:hoeffding-algorithm}

Proposition~\ref{prop:weighted-hoeffding-ranks} reduces the estimator to four
marginal rank vectors, four bivariate rank vectors, and three elementary
symmetric sums. The marginal ranks $r^{(a)}$ and $s^{(a)}$ are prefix sums
after sorting and are produced by Algorithm~\ref{alg:weighted-ranks} with
masses $p_i^a$. The elementary symmetric sums follow from Newton's identities:
with $e_0=1$,
\begin{align*}
    e_k(p_1,\ldots,p_n)
    &= \frac{1}{k}\sum_{a=1}^k(-1)^{a-1}
       e_{k-a}(p_1,\ldots,p_n)\sum_{i=1}^n p_i^a,
\end{align*}
which is linear in $n$ for $k\leq5$.

The bivariate ranks are the only nontrivial step. Computing each $q_i^{(a)}$
directly costs $O(n^2)$. Algorithm~\ref{alg:weighted-bivariate-ranks} instead
computes all of them for one set of masses by a dominance merge sort, the same
device that underlies Knight's algorithm for Kendall's tau. The records are
first sorted by $X$. Each recursive call returns its records sorted by $Y$.
When a record from the right half is emitted during the merge, the accumulated
mass of smaller-$Y$ records from the left half is added to its rank. Those
records also precede it in $X$, so the update counts exactly the newly exposed
lower-left quadrant. The strict comparison in line~\ref{alg:dominance-strict}
makes equal $Y$ values noncontributing. Each pass costs $O(n\log n)$ time and
$O(n)$ memory, and four passes with masses $p_i,p_i^2,p_i^3,p_i^4$ retain
that complexity. Fast rank implementations compute the unweighted statistic in
$O(n\log n)$ time as well \citep{evenzohar2020}. The step that is specific to
the weighted case is the combination of the four mass powers with the
elementary symmetric normalizers: a single pass with $p_i$ would leave the
repeated-index contributions in the order-three to order-five products, and
the higher powers remove precisely those diagonals.

\begin{algorithm}[ht]
  \caption{Weighted bivariate ranks by dominance merge sort}
  \label{alg:weighted-bivariate-ranks}
  \small
  \begin{algorithmic}[1]
    \Procedure{WeightedBivariateRanks}{$(X_i,Y_i,u_i)_{i=1}^n$}
      \State Sort the records by increasing $X_i$
      \State $\mathcal Z\gets$ the sorted record sequence
      \State Set $q_i\gets0$ for every record
      \State $\mathcal Z\gets\Call{DominanceMerge}{\mathcal Z}$
      \State \Return $(q_i)_{i=1}^n$ in the original observation order
    \EndProcedure
    \Statex
    \Function{DominanceMerge}{$\mathcal Z$}
      \If{$|\mathcal Z|\leq1$}
        \State \Return $\mathcal Z$
      \EndIf
      \State Split $\mathcal Z$ into consecutive $X$-order halves
        $\mathcal Z_L,\mathcal Z_R$
      \State $\mathcal L\gets\Call{DominanceMerge}{\mathcal Z_L}$
        \Comment{sorted by $Y$}
      \State $\mathcal R\gets\Call{DominanceMerge}{\mathcal Z_R}$
        \Comment{sorted by $Y$}
      \State $P_{\mathrm{passed}}\gets0$, $\mathcal M\gets$ empty sequence
      \While{$\mathcal L$ and $\mathcal R$ are both nonempty}
        \If{$Y_{\mathcal L_1}<Y_{\mathcal R_1}$}
          \label{alg:dominance-strict}
          \State Append $\mathcal L_1$ to $\mathcal M$,
            $P_{\mathrm{passed}}\gets P_{\mathrm{passed}}+u_{\mathcal L_1}$,
            and remove $\mathcal L_1$
        \Else
          \State $q_{\mathcal R_1}\gets q_{\mathcal R_1}+P_{\mathrm{passed}}$
          \State Append $\mathcal R_1$ to $\mathcal M$ and remove
            $\mathcal R_1$
        \EndIf
      \EndWhile
      \ForAll{records remaining in $\mathcal R$, in order}
        \State $q_{\mathcal R_1}\gets q_{\mathcal R_1}+P_{\mathrm{passed}}$
        \State Append $\mathcal R_1$ to $\mathcal M$ and remove
          $\mathcal R_1$
      \EndFor
      \State Append the records remaining in $\mathcal L$ to $\mathcal M$
      \State \Return $\mathcal M$
    \EndFunction
  \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:weighted-hoeffding} assembles the calculation. Normalizing
the supplied masses is unnecessary because each homogeneous term is divided by
a normalizer of the same degree. The algorithm consists of a fixed number of
sorts, merge passes, and linear scans, so its running time is $O(n\log n)$ and
its auxiliary memory is $O(n)$. By
Proposition~\ref{prop:weighted-hoeffding-ranks}, its output equals the
distinct-index estimator exactly.

\begin{algorithm}[ht]
  \caption{Fast weighted Hoeffding estimator}
  \label{alg:weighted-hoeffding}
  \small
  \begin{algorithmic}[1]
    \Procedure{WeightedHoeffding}{$(X_i,Y_i,p_i)_{i=1}^n$}
      \State Compute $r^{(1)}$ and $s^{(1)}$ by weighted strict-rank scans
        with masses $p_i$
      \State Compute $r^{(2)}$ and $s^{(2)}$ by weighted strict-rank scans
        with masses $p_i^2$
      \For{$a=1,\ldots,4$}
        \State $q^{(a)}\gets
          \Call{WeightedBivariateRanks}{(X_i,Y_i,p_i^a)_{i=1}^n}$
      \EndFor
      \State Compute $e_3(p_1,\ldots,p_n)$, $e_4(p_1,\ldots,p_n)$, and
        $e_5(p_1,\ldots,p_n)$ by Newton's identities
      \State Accumulate $A_1,A_2,A_3$ in one scan using
        Proposition~\ref{prop:weighted-hoeffding-ranks}
      \State \Return
        $30\left\{A_1/(3!e_3)-2A_2/(4!e_4)+A_3/(5!e_5)\right\}$
    \EndProcedure
  \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Properties and limitations}

The rank representation makes several finite-sample properties immediate. The
statistic is invariant under a common positive scaling of the weights, under
permutations of the observations, under strictly increasing transformations
of either margin, and under exchange of $X$ and $Y$. Zero-mass observations
can be removed before computation.

Two limitations should be kept in mind. First, the method assumes continuous
marginals. A tie-corrected weighted Hoeffding coefficient would need a
deliberate fractional convention for bivariate ranks and corresponding
diagonal identities, because the rank representation of
Proposition~\ref{prop:weighted-hoeffding-ranks} fails if midranks are applied
silently. Second, highly concentrated
masses make $e_5(p_1,\ldots,p_n)$ small and the estimator unstable even when
it is formally defined. The asymptotic test in
Section~\ref{subsec:hoeffding-test} therefore requires diffuse weights in
addition to five positive masses.
