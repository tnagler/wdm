\section{Standard weighted dependence measures}
\label{sec:standard-measures}

This section records four familiar coefficients in the case-weight notation of
Section~\ref{sec:framework}. The formulas fix conventions and provide
comparators for the less standard developments in
Sections~\ref{sec:weighted-hoeffding} and~\ref{sec:weighted-chatterjee}. Every
coefficient below is symmetric in the two variables, invariant under a common
positive rescaling of the weights, and unaffected by zero-mass observations.
Table~\ref{tab:standard-measures} summarizes the section; the corresponding
independence tests are treated in Section~\ref{subsec:standard-tests}.

\begin{table}[ht]
  \centering
  \caption{Standard case-weighted dependence measures.}
  \label{tab:standard-measures}
  \begin{tabular}{llll}
    \toprule
    Measure & Basic object & Tie convention & Time \\
    \midrule
    Pearson & weighted moments & not applicable & $O(n)$ \\
    Spearman & weighted average ranks & pair-mass average & $O(n\log n)$ \\
    Kendall & weighted concordant pairs & tau-b denominator & $O(n\log n)$ \\
    Blomqvist & matching median quadrants & pair-mass center & $O(n\log n)$ \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

\subsection{Pearson correlation and Spearman's rho}

With the weighted means $\bar X_w = \sum_{i=1}^n p_iX_i$ and
$\bar Y_w = \sum_{i=1}^n p_iY_i$, the weighted Pearson coefficient is
\begin{align*}
    \widehat\rho_{P,w}
    &= \frac{\sum_{i=1}^n p_i(X_i-\bar X_w)(Y_i-\bar Y_w)}
            {\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^n p_i(X_i-\bar X_w)^2}
             \sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^n p_i(Y_i-\bar Y_w)^2}}.
\end{align*}
It is undefined if either weighted variance is zero and reduces to the
classical product-moment coefficient \citep{pearson1895} under uniform
weights.

Weighted Spearman correlation is the weighted Pearson correlation of the
average scores $R_i^X$ and $R_i^Y$ from Section~\ref{subsec:weighted-ranks},
\begin{align*}
    \widehat\rho_{S,w}
    &= \frac{\sum_{i=1}^n p_i(R_i^X-\bar R_w^X)(R_i^Y-\bar R_w^Y)}
            {\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^n p_i(R_i^X-\bar R_w^X)^2}
             \sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^n p_i(R_i^Y-\bar R_w^Y)^2}},
\end{align*}
where $\bar R_w^X=\sum_{i=1}^n p_iR_i^X$ and
$\bar R_w^Y=\sum_{i=1}^n p_iR_i^Y$. Uniform weights recover the ordinary coefficient with midranks for ties
\citep{spearman1904}. Sorting dominates the computation, so the coefficient
costs $O(n\log n)$ time and $O(n)$ memory.

\subsection{Kendall's tau}

Kendall's coefficient is based on pairwise concordance \citep{kendall1938},
and each pair of observations receives the product of its two case masses.
With $\operatorname{sgn}(0)=0$, the weighted concordance difference and the
masses of pairs untied in each margin are
\begin{align*}
    S_w
    &= \sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}p_ip_j
       \operatorname{sgn}(X_i-X_j)\operatorname{sgn}(Y_i-Y_j),\\
    Q_{X,w}
    &= \sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}p_ip_j\mathbf{1}(X_i\neq X_j),
    &
    Q_{Y,w}
    &= \sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}p_ip_j\mathbf{1}(Y_i\neq Y_j),
\end{align*}
and the weighted tau-b coefficient is
$\widehat\tau_w = S_w/\sqrt{Q_{X,w}Q_{Y,w}}$. Without ties both denominator
factors equal $e_2(p_1,\ldots,p_n)$, and uniform weights give the ordinary
sample tau-b. Multiplicative pair weights also appear in other weighted Kendall
constructions \citep{shieh1998}, which need not share the case-weight
interpretation.

Direct evaluation is quadratic. \citet{knight1966} computes the ordinary
coefficient in $O(n\log n)$ time by counting discordant pairs during a merge
sort, and \citet{lin2018} give a method of the same complexity for an
approximate weighted Kendall correlation. For product case weights, Knight's
merge needs only one modification: when a record of mass $p_j$ is taken from
the right half, its contribution is $p_j$ times the mass still unmerged in the
left half, which sums $p_ip_j$ over the newly exposed discordant pairs. An
initial lexicographic sort by $(X,Y)$ prevents $X$ ties from becoming
inversions, equal $Y$ values are treated as non-inversions, and the tie masses
in the denominator come from linear group scans. The weighted dominance merge
in Section~\ref{sec:weighted-hoeffding} uses the same idea, so we do not
repeat the details here.

\subsection{Blomqvist's beta}

Blomqvist's beta \citep{blomqvist1950} records whether observations fall in
matching quadrants about the marginal centers. The weighted center is defined
through the average scores. Sort the observations by $X$ and let
$R_{(k)}^X$ be the average score of the $k$th ordered value. With $k_X$ the
smallest index $k$ such that $R_{(k)}^X\geq e_2(p_1,\ldots,p_n)$, the center
is
\begin{align*}
    m_{X,w}
    &=
    \begin{cases}
        X_{(k_X)},
        & R_{(k_X)}^X = e_2(p_1,\ldots,p_n),\\
        \bigl(X_{(k_X-1)}+X_{(k_X)}\bigr)/2,
        & R_{(k_X)}^X > e_2(p_1,\ldots,p_n),
    \end{cases}
\end{align*}
and $m_{Y,w}$ is defined analogously. This pair-mass center agrees with the
ordinary sample median under uniform weights; it is not interchangeable with
every convention called a weighted median. The sample coefficient is
\begin{align*}
    \widehat\beta_w
    &= 2\sum_{i=1}^n p_i
       \left\{
         \mathbf{1}(X_i\leq m_{X,w},\,Y_i\leq m_{Y,w})
         +
         \mathbf{1}(X_i>m_{X,w},\,Y_i>m_{Y,w})
       \right\}-1,
\end{align*}
where the lower-left quadrant includes observations on either center and the
upper-right quadrant is strict. For continuous marginals, the population
target is the copula functional $4C(1/2,1/2)-1$. Computing both centers by
sorting takes $O(n\log n)$ time.
