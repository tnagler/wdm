\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Measures of dependence reduce different aspects of a joint distribution to a
small number of interpretable summaries. Pearson's correlation quantifies
linear association, while the rank coefficients of \citet{spearman1904} and
\citet{kendall1938} describe monotone association and are invariant under
strictly increasing marginal transformations. Blomqvist's beta gives a
particularly simple quadrant-based summary \citep{blomqvist1950}. These
coefficients are useful and easy to compute, but none characterizes general
dependence. Hoeffding's rank statistic instead compares the joint distribution
with the product of its marginals and yields a consistent test of independence
under broad conditions \citep{hoeffding1948}. More recently,
\citet{chatterjee2021} introduced a directional rank coefficient that is zero
if and only if the variables are independent and reaches one when the response
is almost surely a measurable function of the predictor.

Many applications attach a nonnegative weight to every observation. Examples
include survey and inverse-probability weights, importance weights, and local
weights used to focus an analysis on a subpopulation. For means and
covariances, weighting amounts to replacing the empirical distribution by a
weighted empirical distribution. For rank statistics of order two or higher,
this substitution is not enough. Products containing repeated observations
must be removed, ties require compatible weighted counts, and the variability
of a weighted statistic depends on the concentration of the weights rather
than only on the nominal sample size. In particular, the familiar effective
sample size of \citet{kish1965} is exact for the conditional variance of a
weighted mean, but it is not automatically the complete variance correction
for nonlinear rank statistics.

This report develops a common case-weight framework for several dependence
measures and concentrates on three less standard contributions. First, it
derives a finite-sample version of Hoeffding's statistic based on products of
weights over distinct observations. The apparently high-order sums can be
written in terms of marginal ranks, bivariate ranks, powers of the weights,
and elementary symmetric weight sums. Weighted bivariate ranks are then
computed by sorting and inversion counting, giving an
$O(n\log n)$ algorithm instead of direct enumeration. Second, the report
states an asymptotic framework for weighted independence tests, separating
ordinary Gaussian limits from the degenerate null limit of Hoeffding's
statistic and making explicit where effective sample size and tie corrections
enter. Third, it studies a weighted extension of Chatterjee's coefficient and
derives its conditional permutation-null mean and variance for a continuous
response.

The formulas are implemented in the header-only C++ library \texttt{wdm} and
are exposed to most users through its R interface. The software serves as a
reproducible implementation of the methods; API details are intentionally left
to the package documentation. Section~\ref{sec:framework} fixes notation and
the interpretation of the weights. Section~\ref{sec:standard-measures}
records the standard weighted coefficients used for comparison.
Sections~4--6 develop weighted Hoeffding computation, independence-test
asymptotics, and weighted Chatterjee correlation. Section~7 assesses the
identities, computational scaling, null calibration, and power numerically.
