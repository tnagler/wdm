\section{Independence tests}
\label{sec:independence-tests}

\subsection{Null hypothesis, weights, and calibration}
\label{subsec:test-framework}

All tests in this section concern

\begin{align*}
  H_0
  &: \mathbb{P}_{XY}=\mathbb{P}_X\otimes\mathbb{P}_Y.
\end{align*}

For an asymptotic sequence of normalized masses, write

\begin{align*}
  s_{r,n}
  &= \sum_{i=1}^n p_i^r,
  &
  n_{\mathrm{eff}}
  &= s_{2,n}^{-1}.
\end{align*}

Unless stated otherwise, analytic limits for the symmetric statistics assume
that the observations are independent and identically distributed and that
the weights are deterministic, or random but independent of all the
observations.  We condition on the weights and require

\begin{align}
  s_{2,n}
  &\longrightarrow0,
  &
  \frac{\max_i p_i}{\sqrt{s_{2,n}}}
  &\longrightarrow0.
  \label{eq:weight-diffuseness}
\end{align}

The second condition is the Lindeberg condition from
Section~\ref{sec:framework}.  It implies, for example,

\begin{align*}
  \frac{s_{4,n}}{s_{2,n}^2}
  &\leq
  \frac{(\max_i p_i)^2}{s_{2,n}}
  \longrightarrow0.
\end{align*}

This distinction between exogenous and observation-dependent masses is
essential.  If $p_i$ is proportional to a function of $X_i$, reweighting
preserves the null factorization, but a squared-weight law enters the sampling
variance and Kish scaling need not give a pivotal statistic.  The weighted
Chatterjee result below is different: it conditions on the predictor order
and masses and therefore permits masses that are functions of the predictor.
Weights that use the responses, or jointly use both variables, are not covered
by either argument.

There is a finite-sample alternative whenever the weights are fixed or depend
only on the predictors.  Conditional on $(X_i,p_i)_{i=1}^n$ and on the
unordered responses, the response assignment is uniform under $H_0$.
Recomputing a statistic over random response permutations therefore gives an
exact conditional test, including for concentrated weights or ties.  The
analytic results below avoid this repeated computation, but their stated
conditions must then be respected.

For a statistic with a standard normal null limit, the upper-, lower-, and
two-sided asymptotic p-values are respectively

\begin{align*}
  p_{>}
  &= 1-\Phi(Z),
  &
  p_{<}
  &= \Phi(Z),
  &
  p_{\neq}
  &= 2\Phi(-|Z|).
\end{align*}

Hoeffding's test is instead an upper-tail test against nondirectional
dependence.  Referring to it as ``two-sided'' means only that both increasing
and decreasing, as well as nonmonotone, alternatives can make the statistic
large; it does not mean that both tails of its null distribution are used.

\subsection{Pearson, Spearman, Kendall, and Blomqvist tests}
\label{subsec:standard-tests}

The first-order null theory of the four standard coefficients is classical.
It is recorded here both to collect all tests in one section and to distinguish
the limiting statistics from finite-sample transformations used by the
software. A hat denotes the corresponding sample coefficient defined in
Section~\ref{sec:standard-measures}.

\begin{proposition}[First-order weighted null limits]
\label{prop:standard-null-limits}
Assume \eqref{eq:weight-diffuseness}, exogenous weights, $H_0$, and continuous,
nondegenerate marginals.  For Pearson's coefficient, also assume positive
finite marginal variances and moments sufficient for the weighted Lindeberg
condition; for Blomqvist's coefficient, assume unique regular marginal
medians.  Then

\begin{align*}
  \sqrt{n_{\mathrm{eff}}}\,\widehat\rho_{P,w}
  &\mathrel{\Longrightarrow} N(0,1),\\
  \sqrt{n_{\mathrm{eff}}}\,\widehat\rho_{S,w}
  &\mathrel{\Longrightarrow} N(0,1),\\
  \frac{3}{2}\sqrt{n_{\mathrm{eff}}}\,\widehat\tau_w
  &\mathrel{\Longrightarrow} N(0,1),\\
  \sqrt{n_{\mathrm{eff}}}\,\widehat\beta_w
  &\mathrel{\Longrightarrow} N(0,1).
\end{align*}
\end{proposition}

To see the constants, standardize $X$ and $Y$ for Pearson's coefficient and
write $U=F_X(X)$ and $V=F_Y(Y)$ for the rank coefficients.  Their first-order
null scores are

\begin{align*}
  \psi_P(X,Y)
  &= \frac{X-\mathbb{E}X}{\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(X)}}
     \frac{Y-\mathbb{E}Y}{\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(Y)}},\\
  \psi_S(U,V)
  &= 12(U-1/2)(V-1/2),\\
  \psi_K(U,V)
  &= 2(2U-1)(2V-1),\\
  \psi_B(U,V)
  &= \{2\mathbf{1}(U\leq1/2)-1\}
     \{2\mathbf{1}(V\leq1/2)-1\}.
\end{align*}

Independence gives

\begin{align*}
  \operatorname{Var}(\psi_P)
  &= 1,
  &
  \operatorname{Var}(\psi_S)
  &= 1,
  &
  \operatorname{Var}(\psi_K)
  &= \frac{4}{9},
  &
  \operatorname{Var}(\psi_B)
  &= 1.
\end{align*}

The weighted asymptotic linear representations are
$\widehat\theta_w=\sum_i p_i\psi(Z_i)+o_p(\sqrt{s_{2,n}})$.
The triangular-array central limit theorem proves the proposition.  For
Spearman's coefficient the displayed score is the influence function of its
copula functional; for Kendall's coefficient it is twice the first projection
of the concordance kernel.  Estimation of either median in Blomqvist's
coefficient has no first-order effect under independence because the opposite
marginal sign has mean zero.  These calculations also show why the four
limits require more than mechanically replacing $n$ by
$n_{\mathrm{eff}}$ when the weights depend on the observations.

The software applies the following finite-sample transformations, with
$N=n_{\mathrm{eff}}$:

\begin{align*}
  Z_P^{\mathrm{app}}
  &= \operatorname{atanh}(\widehat\rho_{P,w})\sqrt{N-3},\\
  Z_S^{\mathrm{app}}
  &= \operatorname{atanh}(\widehat\rho_{S,w})
     \sqrt{\frac{N-3}{1.06}},\\
  Z_B^{\mathrm{app}}
  &= \operatorname{atanh}(\widehat\beta_w)\sqrt N.
\end{align*}

The Pearson and Spearman formulas require $N>3$, and every displayed
$\operatorname{atanh}$ transformation requires its coefficient to lie
strictly between $-1$ and $1$. Outside those domains the corresponding
analytic calibration is unavailable.

The Pearson transformation is Fisher's normal-theory approximation
\citep{fisher1915}; the Blomqvist transformation is first-order equivalent to
the statistic in Proposition~\ref{prop:standard-null-limits}.  The factor
$1.06$ is the finite-sample approximation of
\citet{fieller1957}, not part of the distribution-free Spearman limit.  In
fact,

\begin{align*}
  Z_S^{\mathrm{app}}
  &= \frac{1}{\sqrt{1.06}}
     \sqrt N\,\widehat\rho_{S,w}+o_p(1),
\end{align*}

so its normal calibration is slightly conservative in the limiting framework
of Proposition~\ref{prop:standard-null-limits}.

For reference, the tie-free weighted Kendall numerator has an exact
conditional null variance.  With $S_w$ as in
Section~\ref{sec:standard-measures},

\begin{align*}
  \operatorname{Var}_0(S_w\mid p)
  &= \sum_{i<j}p_i^2p_j^2
     +\frac{2}{9}\sum_{i<j<k}
       p_ip_jp_k(p_i+p_j+p_k).
\end{align*}

Each pair kernel has variance one, disjoint pair kernels are independent, and
two kernels that share one observation have covariance $1/9$.  Since
$\widehat\tau_w=S_w/e_2(p_1,\ldots,p_n)$ without ties, division by
$e_2(p)^2$ gives the exact variance of $\widehat\tau_w$ and, under
\eqref{eq:weight-diffuseness},

\begin{align*}
  \operatorname{Var}_0(\widehat\tau_w\mid p)
  &\sim \frac{4}{9}s_{2,n}.
\end{align*}

With ties, the ordinary conditional permutation variance depends on the two
sets of marginal tie multiplicities \citep{kendall1945,kendall1947}. The
implementation extends that formula by rescaling the masses to fractional
pseudo-counts $q_i=Np_i$ and substituting weighted pair and triplet masses for
integer tie counts.  It is exact for uniform masses and first-order equivalent
to Proposition~\ref{prop:standard-null-limits} without ties; for unequal
masses it is a finite-sample approximation, not a new exact permutation
identity. For uniform masses, only the conditional variance is exact after
conditioning on both marginal multisets; use of a normal tail remains
asymptotic. The full algebra is recorded in
Appendix~\ref{app:null-asymptotics}.

\subsection{The degenerate null limit for weighted Hoeffding D}
\label{subsec:hoeffding-test}

Hoeffding's estimator is unbiased under $H_0$, so its null mean is zero. Its
first projection also vanishes, which makes the statistic second-order
degenerate and rules out a Gaussian limit at the $\sqrt{n_{\mathrm{eff}}}$
scale. \citet{hoeffding1948} derived the centered degenerate null limit;
\citet{blum1961} tabulated the equivalent nonnegative quadratic form used for
calibration.

\begin{theorem}[Weighted Hoeffding null limit]
\label{thm:weighted-hoeffding-null}
For each $n$, let $(p_1,\ldots,p_n)$ be a deterministic mass vector with at
least five positive entries, and let $(X_i,Y_i)_{i=1}^n$ be independent and
identically distributed. Suppose the marginals are continuous and
\eqref{eq:weight-diffuseness} holds along the triangular array. Under $H_0$,

\begin{align}
  \frac{1-s_{2,n}}{s_{2,n}}\frac{\widehat D_w}{30}
  &\mathrel{\Longrightarrow}
  \sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}
  \frac{G_{jk}^2-1}{\pi^4j^2k^2},
  \label{eq:hoeffding-centered-limit}
\end{align}

where the $G_{jk}$ are independent standard normal variables.  Equivalently,

\begin{align}
  \mathcal H_{n,w}
  &= \frac{\pi^4}{2}
     \left\{
       (n_{\mathrm{eff}}-1)\frac{\widehat D_w}{30}
       +\frac{1}{36}
     \right\}
  \mathrel{\Longrightarrow}
  \mathcal H
  = \frac12\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}
    \frac{G_{jk}^2}{j^2k^2}.
  \label{eq:hoeffding-positive-limit}
\end{align}

Moreover,

\begin{align*}
  \mathbb{E}_0(\widehat D_w\mid p)
  &=0,\\
  \operatorname{Var}_0(\widehat D_w\mid p)
  &= \frac{2}{9}
     \frac{s_{2,n}^2-s_{4,n}}{(1-s_{2,n})^2}
     +o(s_{2,n}^2).
\end{align*}
\end{theorem}

The same conclusions hold conditionally, path by path, for random exogenous
weights whose realized triangular array satisfies the assumptions. The test
rejects at asymptotic level $\alpha$ when $\mathcal H_{n,w}$ exceeds the
$(1-\alpha)$ quantile of $\mathcal H$. This limit law is continuous: condition
on every Gaussian variable except $G_{11}$ and use the continuity of a
noncentral squared-normal distribution. Under a fixed alternative with
$D>0$, the weighted law of large numbers for each distinct-tuple component
gives $\widehat D_w\to_p D$, so $\mathcal H_{n,w}\to_p\infty$ and the test is
consistent.

\begin{proof}
Apply the probability integral transform and write
$Z_i=(U_i,V_i)$, where all coordinates are independent uniform variables.
Let $T(P)=D(P)/30$ and let $P_0$ be uniform measure on $[0,1]^2$.  For
$z=(u,v)$, contamination in the direction $\delta_z-P_0$ changes the
joint-minus-product discrepancy at $(x,y)$ at the rate

\begin{align*}
  \dot\Delta_z(x,y)
  &= \{\mathbf{1}(u\leq x)-x\}
     \{\mathbf{1}(v\leq y)-y\}.
\end{align*}

Because the discrepancy itself is zero at $P_0$, the first derivative of
$T$ vanishes. The change in the integrating measure contributes only at third
order, while the mixed second derivative is

\begin{align*}
  K(z,z')
  &= \int_0^1\int_0^1
     \dot\Delta_z(x,y)\dot\Delta_{z'}(x,y)
     \,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\\
  &= k(u,u')k(v,v').
\end{align*}

More precisely, let $\nu$ and $\gamma$ be zero-mass signed directions with
bounded densities relative to $P_0$. Then

\begin{align*}
  \dot\Delta_\nu(x,y)
  &=\int \dot\Delta_z(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}\nu(z),
\end{align*}

and hence

\begin{align*}
  \left.
  \frac{\partial^2}{\partial\varepsilon\,\partial\eta}
  T(P_0+\varepsilon\nu+\eta\gamma)
  \right|_{\varepsilon=\eta=0}
  &=2\int\!\!\int K(z,z')\,\mathrm{d}\nu(z)\,\mathrm{d}\gamma(z').
\end{align*}

The kernel $K$ is canonical in either argument. Therefore equality of these
bilinear forms over bounded zero-mean densities identifies the canonical
$L^2(P_0^2)$ quadratic-derivative kernel. Direct integration gives

\begin{align*}
  k(a,b)
  &= \int_0^1
     \{\mathbf{1}(a\leq t)-t\}
     \{\mathbf{1}(b\leq t)-t\}\,\mathrm{d}t\\
  &= \frac{a^2+b^2}{2}-\max(a,b)+\frac13.
\end{align*}

To connect this derivative with the distinct-index statistic, symmetrize the
order-$m$ kernels defining $\widehat\theta_{m,w}$ and denote their canonical
order-$r$ projections relative to $P_0$ by $h_{m,r}$. Differentiating
$T=\theta_3-2\theta_4+\theta_5$ once gives the first identity below. The
kernel representing its mixed second derivative is
$2\{3h_{3,2}-12h_{4,2}+10h_{5,2}\}$. Comparison of the two bilinear forms for
all such $\nu$ and $\gamma$ gives the second identity in $L^2(P_0^2)$:

\begin{align*}
  3h_{3,1}-8h_{4,1}+5h_{5,1}
  &=0,\\
  3h_{3,2}-12h_{4,2}+10h_{5,2}
  &=K.
\end{align*}

The first identity proves degeneracy. The second identifies the full
quadratic projection and its normalization.

For completeness, put

\begin{align*}
  c_{m,n}
  &=m!e_m(p_1,\ldots,p_n),\\
  c_{m-r,n}^{(-A)}
  &=(m-r)!e_{m-r}\bigl((p_i)_{i\notin A}\bigr).
\end{align*}

The canonical decomposition of \citet{hoeffding1948u}, with the coefficient
of each index set replaced by its normalized distinct-tuple mass, gives

\begin{align*}
  \widehat\theta_{m,w}-\theta_m
  &= \sum_{r=1}^m
     \sum_{\substack{A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\\|A|=r}}
     \frac{
       (m)_r\left(\prod_{i\in A}p_i\right)
       c_{m-r,n}^{(-A)}
     }{c_{m,n}}
     h_{m,r}(Z_A).
\end{align*}

Here $(m)_r=m!/(m-r)!$. Removing collisions from a fixed number of draws
gives, uniformly over all $A$ with $|A|\leq5$,

\begin{align*}
  |c_{m,n}-1|
  &\leq C_m s_{2,n},\\
  |c_{m-r,n}^{(-A)}-1|
  &\leq C_m\left(s_{2,n}+\sum_{i\in A}p_i\right).
\end{align*}

To make the remainder calculation explicit, let
$\alpha_3=1$, $\alpha_4=-2$, and $\alpha_5=1$, and define

\begin{align*}
  R_{1,n}
  &=\sum_i p_i\sum_{m=3}^5\alpha_m m
    \left\{
      \frac{c_{m-1,n}^{(-\{i\})}}{c_{m,n}}-1
    \right\}h_{m,1}(Z_i),\\
  R_{2,n}
  &=\sum_{i<l}p_ip_l\sum_{m=3}^5\alpha_m(m)_2
    \left\{
      \frac{c_{m-2,n}^{(-\{i,l\})}}{c_{m,n}}
      -\frac{1}{1-s_{2,n}}
    \right\}h_{m,2}(Z_i,Z_l),\\
  R_{\geq3,n}
  &=\sum_{m=3}^5\alpha_m
    \sum_{r=3}^m
    \sum_{\substack{A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\\|A|=r}}
    \frac{
      (m)_r\left(\prod_{i\in A}p_i\right)
      c_{m-r,n}^{(-A)}
    }{c_{m,n}}h_{m,r}(Z_A).
\end{align*}

Substitution of the two projection identities gives

\begin{align}
  \frac{\widehat D_w}{30}
  &= \frac{2}{1-s_{2,n}}
     \sum_{1\leq i<l\leq n}p_ip_lK(Z_i,Z_l)+R_n,
  \label{eq:hoeffding-quadratic-projection}
\end{align}

where $R_n=R_{1,n}+R_{2,n}+R_{\geq3,n}$. Canonical projections of different
orders or different index sets are orthogonal. The collision bounds, the
fixed $L^2(P_0^r)$ norms of the kernels, and
$e_r(p_1^2,\ldots,p_n^2)\leq s_{2,n}^r/r!$ therefore give

\begin{align*}
  \mathbb{E}_0(R_{1,n}^2\mid p)
  &\leq C s_{2,n}(\max_i p_i+s_{2,n})^2,\\
  \mathbb{E}_0(R_{2,n}^2\mid p)
  &\leq C s_{2,n}^2(\max_i p_i+s_{2,n})^2,\\
  \mathbb{E}_0(R_{\geq3,n}^2\mid p)
  &\leq C s_{2,n}^3.
\end{align*}

Consequently,

\begin{align*}
  \frac{\mathbb{E}_0(R_n^2\mid p)}{s_{2,n}^2}
  &\leq C\Biggl[
    \left\{
      \frac{\max_i p_i}{\sqrt{s_{2,n}}}+\sqrt{s_{2,n}}
    \right\}^2
    +(\max_i p_i+s_{2,n})^2+s_{2,n}
  \Biggr]
  \longrightarrow0.
\end{align*}

The three displayed terms thus come, respectively, from the residual first
projection, the quadratic coefficient error, and projections of orders three
through five.

It remains to identify the law of the quadratic projection.  The
one-dimensional kernel has the cosine expansion

\begin{align*}
  k(a,b)
  &= \sum_{j=1}^{\infty}
     \frac{2}{\pi^2j^2}\cos(\pi ja)\cos(\pi jb).
\end{align*}

Consequently, $K$ has orthonormal eigenfunctions and eigenvalues

\begin{align*}
  \phi_{jk}(u,v)
  &=2\cos(\pi ju)\cos(\pi kv),
  &
  \lambda_{jk}
  &=\frac{1}{\pi^4j^2k^2}.
\end{align*}

With $a_{i,n}=p_i/\sqrt{s_{2,n}}$, the leading term in
\eqref{eq:hoeffding-quadratic-projection}, multiplied by
$(1-s_{2,n})/s_{2,n}$, is

\begin{align*}
  &\frac{2}{s_{2,n}}\sum_{i<l}p_ip_lK(Z_i,Z_l)\\
  &\qquad=
  \sum_{j,k\geq1}\lambda_{jk}
  \left[
    \left\{\sum_i a_{i,n}\phi_{jk}(Z_i)\right\}^2
    -\sum_i a_{i,n}^2\phi_{jk}(Z_i)^2
  \right].
\end{align*}

For any finite set of indices, the weighted Lindeberg central limit theorem
turns the first sums into independent standard normal variables. Since the
eigenfunctions are bounded and
$\max_i a_{i,n}^2\to0$, the weighted law of large numbers makes each second
sum converge in probability to one.

It remains to justify passage from finite truncations to the full series. Let
$K_M$ retain the eigenpairs with $1\leq j,k\leq M$. Canonicality gives the
uniform identity

\begin{align*}
  &\mathbb{E}_0\!\left[
    \left|
      \frac{2}{s_{2,n}}\sum_{i<l}p_ip_l
      \{K-K_M\}(Z_i,Z_l)
    \right|^2
    \middle|p
  \right]\\
  &\qquad=
  2\left(1-\frac{s_{4,n}}{s_{2,n}^2}\right)
  \sum_{\substack{j>M\text{ or }k>M}}
  \lambda_{jk}^2
  \leq
  2\sum_{\substack{j>M\text{ or }k>M}}
  \lambda_{jk}^2.
\end{align*}

The corresponding tail of the limiting centered Gaussian series has variance
twice the same eigenvalue-square sum. Finally,

\begin{align*}
  \sum_{j,k\geq1}\lambda_{jk}^2
  &=\frac{1}{8100},
\end{align*}

so both tail bounds vanish as $M\to\infty$. This proves
\eqref{eq:hoeffding-centered-limit}.  The identity

\begin{align*}
  \sum_{j,k\geq1}\lambda_{jk}
  &=\frac{1}{36}
\end{align*}

adds back the mean of the quadratic series and proves
\eqref{eq:hoeffding-positive-limit}.

The estimator is exactly unbiased because every normalized distinct tuple has
the same null expectation. Let $Q_n$ denote the quadratic term on the
right-hand side of \eqref{eq:hoeffding-quadratic-projection}. Canonicality
gives

\begin{align*}
  \operatorname{Var}_0\!\left(
    \frac{2}{1-s_{2,n}}\sum_{i<l}p_ip_lK(Z_i,Z_l)
  \middle|p\right)
  &=\frac{s_{2,n}^2-s_{4,n}}
          {4050(1-s_{2,n})^2}.
\end{align*}

In particular, $\operatorname{Var}_0(Q_n\mid p)=O(s_{2,n}^2)$. By
Cauchy--Schwarz,

\begin{align*}
  &\left|
    \operatorname{Var}_0(Q_n+R_n\mid p)
    -\operatorname{Var}_0(Q_n\mid p)
  \right|\\
  &\qquad\leq
  \mathbb{E}_0(R_n^2\mid p)
  +2\sqrt{
    \operatorname{Var}_0(Q_n\mid p)
    \mathbb{E}_0(R_n^2\mid p)
  }
  =o(s_{2,n}^2).
\end{align*}

Multiplication by $30^2$ completes the variance calculation.
\end{proof}

The survival function of $\mathcal H$ has no elementary form. The software
linearly interpolates tabulated probabilities for the
Blum--Kiefer--Rosenblatt limit and uses an exponential formula outside the
table; both steps are numerical approximations inherited from the software
implementation, not finite-sample theorems. Its internal convention replaces the
$1/36$ in \eqref{eq:hoeffding-positive-limit} by
$(1-s_{2,n})/36$.  The difference is $s_{2,n}/36$ before multiplication by
$\pi^4/2$ and is asymptotically negligible.

The theorem does not extend automatically to $p_i\propto w(X_i)$.  For
example, the horizontal covariance kernel then involves

\begin{align*}
  \Gamma_{X,w}(x,x')
  &=\frac{
    \mathbb{E}\!\left[
      w(X)^2
      \{\mathbf{1}(X\leq x)-F_w(x)\}
      \{\mathbf{1}(X\leq x')-F_w(x')\}
    \right]
  }{\mathbb{E}\{w(X)^2\}},
\end{align*}

where $F_w$ is the distribution induced by first-power weights.  The first-
and second-power weight laws generally differ, changing both the eigenvalues
and the centering constant.  Conditional response permutation is the safe
calibration in that setting.

\subsection{Weighted Chatterjee test}
\label{subsec:chatterjee-test}

Put the positive-mass observations in the predictor order used in
Section~\ref{sec:weighted-chatterjee}.  Under $H_0$ with a continuous response,
conditional on that order and the masses,

\begin{align*}
  U_i
  &=F_Y(Y_i),
  &i&=1,\ldots,m,
\end{align*}

are independent uniform variables.  Define their weighted empirical
distribution and the path statistic by

\begin{align*}
  F_w(u)
  &=\sum_{j=1}^m p_j\mathbf{1}(U_j\leq u),\\
  A_w
  &=\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}p_i
    |F_w(U_{i+1})-F_w(U_i)|.
\end{align*}

The reported coefficient is $1-A_w/C_w$.  For inference it is convenient to
use

\begin{align*}
  \widetilde\xi_w
  &=1-3A_w.
\end{align*}

This replacement removes the random weighted-rank denominator and admits a
closed-form conditional projection variance.

\begin{theorem}[Conditional weighted Chatterjee null theory]
\label{thm:weighted-chatterjee-null}
Suppose the observations are independent and identically distributed and the
response is continuous and independent of the predictor. The weights may be
fixed or functions only of the predictor. Conditional on the predictor order,
its response-independent tie breaking, and the $m\geq2$ positive masses, set

\begin{align*}
  s_{2,m}
  &=\sum_{i=1}^m p_i^2,
  &P_E
  &=\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}p_i,
  &
  S_E
  &=\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}p_i^2,\\
  S_A
  &=\sum_{i=1}^{m-2}p_ip_{i+1},
  &
  S_{EN}
  &=\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}p_i(p_i+p_{i+1}).
\end{align*}

Then the conditional mean of $A_w$ is exactly

\begin{align}
  \mu_{A,w}
  &=\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}p_i
    \left\{\frac13+\frac{p_i+p_{i+1}}6\right\}
   =\frac{P_E}{3}+\frac{S_{EN}}6.
  \label{eq:chatterjee-null-mean}
\end{align}

Define the projection variance

\begin{align}
  v_{A,w}^2
  &=\frac{S_E}{18}+\frac{S_A}{90}
    +\frac{P_E^2s_{2,m}}{45}
    -\frac{P_ES_{EN}}{45}.
  \label{eq:chatterjee-projection-variance}
\end{align}

If, along the realized triangular array,

\begin{align*}
  s_{2,m}
  &\longrightarrow0,
  &
  \frac{\max_i p_i}{\sqrt{s_{2,m}}}
  &\longrightarrow0,
\end{align*}

then

\begin{align*}
  \operatorname{Var}_0(A_w\mid X,p)
  &=v_{A,w}^2+o(s_{2,m}),\\
  Z_{\xi,w}
  &=\frac{
      \widetilde\xi_w-{1-3\mu_{A,w}}
    }{3v_{A,w}}
  \mathrel{\Longrightarrow}N(0,1).
\end{align*}

Moreover, $\widehat\xi_w-\widetilde\xi_w=o_p(\sqrt{s_{2,m}})$, so the
reported coefficient and inferential surrogate are first-order equivalent
under the same conditions.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For one predictor edge, set

\begin{align*}
  \Delta_i
  &=|F_w(U_{i+1})-F_w(U_i)|\\
  &=\sum_{j=1}^m p_j
    \mathbf{1}\!\left\{
      \min(U_i,U_{i+1})<U_j
      \leq\max(U_i,U_{i+1})
    \right\}.
\end{align*}

For $j\notin\{i,i+1\}$, each of the three variables is equally likely to be
the middle order statistic, so the indicator has mean $1/3$.  For either
endpoint it has mean $1/2$, because it is included precisely when that
endpoint is the larger one.  Therefore

\begin{align*}
  \mathbb{E}_0(\Delta_i\mid X,p)
  &=(1-p_i-p_{i+1})\frac13
    +(p_i+p_{i+1})\frac12\\
  &=\frac13+\frac{p_i+p_{i+1}}6.
\end{align*}

Multiplication by $p_i$ and summation prove
\eqref{eq:chatterjee-null-mean}.

The projection calculation can be made explicit.  For independent uniform
$U,V,W$, let

\begin{align*}
  d(U,V)
  &=|U-V|,
  &
  \psi(W)
  &=2W(1-W)-\frac13.
\end{align*}

The leading path-and-node projection of $A_w-\mu_{A,w}$ is

\begin{align}
  L_w
  &=\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}p_i\{d(U_i,U_{i+1})-1/3\}
    +P_E\sum_{j=1}^m p_j\psi(U_j).
  \label{eq:chatterjee-projection}
\end{align}

The required moments follow by elementary integration:

\begin{align*}
  \mathbb{E}d(U,V)
  &=\frac13,
  &
  \mathbb{E}d(U,V)^2
  &=\frac16,\\
  \mathbb{E}\{d(U,V)d(V,W)\}
  &=\frac{7}{60},
  &
  \mathbb{E}\psi(U)^2
  &=\frac1{45},\\
  \mathbb{E}\{d(U,V)\psi(U)\}
  &=-\frac1{90}.
\end{align*}

For example,

\begin{align*}
  \mathbb{E}\{d(U,V)\mid U=u\}
  &=u^2-u+\frac12
   =\frac13-\frac12\psi(u),
\end{align*}

which gives the last covariance.  Nonadjacent path terms are independent;
adjacent path terms have covariance
$7/60-(1/3)^2=1/180$.  A path term is correlated with a node term only at
one of its two endpoints, where the covariance is $-1/90$. Expanding
$\operatorname{Var}(L_w\mid X,p)$ now gives

\begin{align*}
  \operatorname{Var}(L_w\mid X,p)
  &=\frac{S_E}{18}+\frac{S_A}{90}
    +\frac{P_E^2s_{2,m}}{45}
    -\frac{P_ES_{EN}}{45},
\end{align*}

which proves \eqref{eq:chatterjee-projection-variance} and explains all four
terms in it.

To control the remainder, define

\begin{align*}
  h(u,v,w)
  &=\mathbf{1}\{\min(u,v)<w\leq\max(u,v)\},\\
  \eta(u,v,w)
  &=h(u,v,w)-d(u,v)-\psi(w).
\end{align*}

Write $q_m=\max_i p_i$ and abbreviate
$\eta_{i,j}=\eta(U_i,U_{i+1},U_j)$. The identity for $\Delta_i$ gives the
exact decomposition

\begin{align*}
  A_w-\mu_{A,w}
  &=L_w+R_{\xi,w},\\
  R_{\xi,w}
  &=\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\sum_{j=1}^m
    p_ip_j\eta_{i,j}-\frac{S_{EN}}6.
\end{align*}

Separate the terms with three distinct labels from the endpoint collisions:

\begin{align*}
  R_{\xi,w}^{\mathrm d}
  &=\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}
    \sum_{j\notin\{i,i+1\}}p_ip_j\eta_{i,j},\\
  R_{\xi,w}^{\mathrm c}
  &=\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}p_i\left[
      p_i\{\eta_{i,i}-1/6\}
      +p_{i+1}\{\eta_{i,i+1}-1/6\}
    \right].
\end{align*}

For three independent uniforms, direct conditioning gives

\begin{align*}
  \mathbb E\{h(U,V,W)\mid U,V\}
  &=d(U,V),\\
  \mathbb E\{h(U,V,W)\mid W=w\}
  &=2w(1-w)=\psi(w)+\frac13,\\
  \mathbb E\{h(U,V,W)\mid U=u\}
  &=\int_0^1|u-v|\,\mathrm{d}v
   =\mathbb E\{d(U,V)\mid U=u\}.
\end{align*}

Since $\mathbb E d(U,V)=1/3$ and $\mathbb E\psi(W)=0$, these identities show
that $\eta(U,V,W)$ has mean zero conditional on any one argument and that
$\mathbb E\{\eta(U,V,W)\mid U,V\}=0$. Each collision has mean $1/6$, because
$\mathbb E h(U,V,U)=\mathbb E h(U,V,V)=1/2$, whereas
$\mathbb E d(U,V)=1/3$ and $\mathbb E\psi(U)=0$. Hence
$R_{\xi,w}=R_{\xi,w}^{\mathrm d}+R_{\xi,w}^{\mathrm c}$ is centered. Moreover,

\begin{align*}
  S_{EN}
  &=\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}p_i(p_i+p_{i+1})
  \leq 2s_{2,m},
\end{align*}

so boundedness of $\eta$ gives the pointwise bound
$|R_{\xi,w}^{\mathrm c}|\leq C s_{2,m}$.

It remains to enumerate products in
$\mathbb E\{(R_{\xi,w}^{\mathrm d})^2\mid X,p\}$. Two distinct-label
summands have zero covariance when their triples share at most one label.
For the same edge, conditioning on its two endpoints also makes different
external-node terms uncorrelated, so only identical triples remain; their
total squared weight is bounded by

\begin{align*}
  \sum_{i=1}^{m-1}p_i^2
    \sum_{j\notin\{i,i+1\}}p_j^2
  &\leq s_{2,m}^2.
\end{align*}

For adjacent edges $i$ and $i+1$, a second shared label can arise only from
a common external node, from $j=i+2$, or from the second external node being
$i$. The corresponding coefficient sums are bounded respectively by

\begin{align*}
  \sum_{i=1}^{m-2}p_ip_{i+1}\sum_jp_j^2
  &\leq s_{2,m}^2,\\
  \sum_{i=1}^{m-2}p_ip_{i+1}p_{i+2}
  &\leq q_m s_{2,m},\\
  \sum_{i=1}^{m-2}p_i^2p_{i+1}
  &\leq q_m s_{2,m}.
\end{align*}

Here and below, enlarging an external-node sum only increases its weight
bound. For disjoint edges $i$ and $k$, two labels are shared only if each
external node is an endpoint of the other edge. Their total coefficient is
at most

\begin{align*}
  \sum_{i,k}p_ip_k(p_i+p_{i+1})(p_k+p_{k+1})
  &\leq4q_m^2,
\end{align*}

where nonexistent outgoing-edge terms are omitted. Since $|\eta|\leq3$,
these exhaustive same-edge, adjacent-edge, and disjoint-edge cases yield

\begin{align*}
  \mathbb{E}_0(R_{\xi,w}^2\mid X,p)
  &\leq C\left\{
    s_{2,m}^2+q_ms_{2,m}+q_m^2
  \right\}
  =o(s_{2,m}).
\end{align*}

Appendix~\ref{app:chatterjee-null-moments} enumerates the same-edge,
adjacent-edge, and disjoint-edge products explicitly and gives the exact
finite conditional variance.

Since $\operatorname{Var}(L_w\mid X,p)=v_{A,w}^2=O(s_{2,m})$,
Cauchy--Schwarz also gives

\begin{align*}
  \left|
    \operatorname{Var}_0(A_w\mid X,p)-v_{A,w}^2
  \right|
  &\leq
    \mathbb E_0(R_{\xi,w}^2\mid X,p)
    +2v_{A,w}
      \sqrt{\mathbb E_0(R_{\xi,w}^2\mid X,p)}\\
  &=o(s_{2,m}),
\end{align*}

which proves the variance assertion in the theorem.

To apply a central limit theorem, write $L_w$ as the sum of

\begin{align*}
  T_{i,w}
  &=p_i\{d(U_i,U_{i+1})-1/3\}+P_Ep_i\psi(U_i),
  &&i=1,\ldots,m-1,\\
  T_{m,w}
  &=P_Ep_m\psi(U_m).
\end{align*}

This is a row-wise one-dependent triangular array of centered, bounded
variables. Its variance is comparable to $s_{2,m}$; one transparent positive
form is

\begin{align*}
  v_{A,w}^2
  &=\frac{2}{45}\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}p_i^2
    +\frac1{180}\sum_{j=1}^m
      \{p_{j-1}\mathbf{1}(j>1)
        +p_j\mathbf{1}(j<m)-2P_Ep_j\}^2.
\end{align*}

In the last display, an edge-weight term is omitted when its indicated edge
does not exist. The elementary bound

\begin{align*}
  \sum_{j=1}^m
    \{p_{j-1}\mathbf 1(j>1)+p_j\mathbf 1(j<m)-2P_Ep_j\}^2
  &\leq18s_{2,m}
\end{align*}

and $p_m^2\leq q_m^2=o(s_{2,m})$ show that

\begin{align*}
  \frac{2}{45}(s_{2,m}-p_m^2)
  &\leq v_{A,w}^2
  \leq C s_{2,m}.
\end{align*}

Thus $v_{A,w}^2\asymp s_{2,m}$. Also
$|T_{i,w}|\leq Cp_i$, so

\begin{align*}
  \max_i\frac{|T_{i,w}|}{v_{A,w}}
  &\leq C\frac{q_m}{\sqrt{s_{2,m}}}
  \longrightarrow0.
\end{align*}

For every $\varepsilon>0$, the indicators in the row-wise Lindeberg sum

\begin{align*}
  \frac1{v_{A,w}^2}\sum_{i=1}^m
  \mathbb E_0\left[
    T_{i,w}^2\mathbf 1\{|T_{i,w}|>\varepsilon v_{A,w}\}
    \middle|X,p
  \right]
\end{align*}

are therefore all zero for sufficiently large $m$. The fixed-dependence
triangular-array theorem of \citet[Theorem~1.1]{janson2021} now gives
$L_w/v_{A,w}\Longrightarrow N(0,1)$. The $L^2$ remainder bound gives
$R_{\xi,w}/v_{A,w}\to_p0$, and

\begin{align*}
  \frac{\widetilde\xi_w-(1-3\mu_{A,w})}{3v_{A,w}}
  &=-\frac{A_w-\mu_{A,w}}{v_{A,w}}
  \mathrel{\Longrightarrow}N(0,1),
\end{align*}

which proves the stated normal limit.

Finally, in response-rank order, $C_w$ is a Riemann sum for

\begin{align*}
  2\int_0^1u(1-u)\,\mathrm{d}u
  &=\frac13.
\end{align*}

The integrand is Lipschitz, so
$|C_w-1/3|\leq2\max_i p_i$.  Since $A_w\leq1$, diffuseness implies

\begin{align*}
  \widehat\xi_w-\widetilde\xi_w
  &=A_w\left(3-C_w^{-1}\right)
  =o_p(\sqrt{s_{2,m}}),
\end{align*}

which completes the proof.
\end{proof}

The exact centering in the implemented continuous-response inferential
surrogate is $1-3\mu_{A,w}$ and its standard error is $3v_{A,w}$. Formula
\eqref{eq:chatterjee-projection-variance} is a projection variance: it is
asymptotically equivalent to the exact variance under the theorem, but is not
the exact finite-permutation variance.  For uniform masses it recovers the
familiar limiting variance $2/(5m)$, whereas the exact finite variance of the
ordinary reported coefficient is

\begin{align*}
  \operatorname{Var}_0(\widehat\xi)
  &=\frac{(m-2)(4m-7)}{10(m-1)^2(m+1)}.
\end{align*}

The natural Chatterjee alternative is upper-tailed.  A two-sided normal
p-value can be reported, but a lower-tail rejection should not be interpreted
as ``negative association,'' because the population coefficient is
nonnegative and directional.

If the response is discrete or tied, the library retains
\eqref{eq:weighted-chatterjee} as the reported coefficient.  For uniform
masses it uses the known tied-response asymptotic variance of
\citet{chatterjee2021}; the formula is given in
Appendix~\ref{app:chatterjee-null-moments}.  Unequally weighted analytic
inference for a tied or discrete response is not justified by
Theorem~\ref{thm:weighted-chatterjee-null} and is deliberately left
unavailable; conditional permutation remains valid when the weights are fixed
or predictor-dependent.
