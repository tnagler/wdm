\section{Weighted Hoeffding's \texorpdfstring{$D$}{D} and Chatterjee's
\texorpdfstring{$\xi$}{xi}}
\label{sec:nonstandard-measures}

\subsection{Weighted Hoeffding's dependence measure}
\label{sec:weighted-hoeffding}

\subsubsection{Definition and finite-sample representation}

Let $H$ denote the joint distribution function of $(X,Y)$ and let $F_X$ and
$F_Y$ denote its marginals. Throughout this section the marginal
distributions are assumed continuous. The normalized version of Hoeffding's
population coefficient used here is

\begin{align*}
  D
  &= 30\int
     \left\{H(x,y)-F_X(x)F_Y(y)\right\}^2\,\mathrm{d}H(x,y)\\
  &= 30\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[
     \left\{H(X,Y)-F_X(X)F_Y(Y)\right\}^2
     \right].
\end{align*}

The coefficient is zero under independence. The factor 30 gives value one
under a strictly increasing or decreasing functional relation. Hoeffding's
original test is based on an unbiased rank estimator of this functional
\citep{hoeffding1948}.

The expansion of the square involves expectations of orders three, four, and
five. To make the distinct-observation structure explicit, let
$(X^{(j)},Y^{(j)})$, $j=0,\ldots,4$, be independent copies of $(X,Y)$ and set

\begin{align*}
  I_j^X
  &= \mathbf{1}\!\left(X^{(j)}<X^{(0)}\right),
  &
  I_j^Y
  &= \mathbf{1}\!\left(Y^{(j)}<Y^{(0)}\right),
  &
  I_j^{XY}
  &= I_j^X I_j^Y.
\end{align*}

Then

\begin{align*}
  \frac{D}{30}
  &= \theta_3-2\theta_4+\theta_5,\\
  \theta_3
  &= \mathbb{E}\!\left[I_1^{XY}I_2^{XY}\right],\\
  \theta_4
  &= \mathbb{E}\!\left[I_1^{XY}I_2^X I_3^Y\right],\\
  \theta_5
  &= \mathbb{E}\!\left[I_1^X I_2^X I_3^Y I_4^Y\right].
\end{align*}

This representation suggests a weighted estimator without repeated sample
indices. For observations $i$ and $j$, write

\begin{align*}
  I_{j,i}^X
  &= \mathbf{1}(X_j<X_i),
  &
  I_{j,i}^Y
  &= \mathbf{1}(Y_j<Y_i),
  &
  I_{j,i}^{XY}
  &= I_{j,i}^X I_{j,i}^Y.
\end{align*}

Using the normalized case masses from Section~\ref{sec:framework}, define

\begin{align*}
  \widehat\theta_{3,w}
  &= \frac{
       \displaystyle
       \sum_{i,j,k}^{\neq}p_ip_jp_k I_{j,i}^{XY}I_{k,i}^{XY}
     }{3!e_3(p_1,\ldots,p_n)},\\
  \widehat\theta_{4,w}
  &= \frac{
       \displaystyle
       \sum_{i,j,k,l}^{\neq}
       p_ip_jp_kp_l I_{j,i}^{XY}I_{k,i}^X I_{l,i}^Y
     }{4!e_4(p_1,\ldots,p_n)},\\
  \widehat\theta_{5,w}
  &= \frac{
       \displaystyle
       \sum_{i,j,k,l,m}^{\neq}
       p_ip_jp_kp_lp_m I_{j,i}^X I_{k,i}^X I_{l,i}^Y I_{m,i}^Y
     }{5!e_5(p_1,\ldots,p_n)},
\end{align*}

where $\sum^{\neq}$ means that every displayed index is distinct. In the term
of order $k$, the denominator is $k!e_k(p_1,\ldots,p_n)$. This is exactly the
total mass of all ordered distinct $k$-tuples. The weighted Hoeffding estimator
is

\begin{align*}
  \widehat D_w
  &= 30\left(
       \widehat\theta_{3,w}
       -2\widehat\theta_{4,w}
       +\widehat\theta_{5,w}
     \right).
\end{align*}

At least five observations must have positive mass. If the observations are
independent and identically distributed and the weights are deterministic or
independent of the observed values, each $\widehat\theta_{k,w}$ is unbiased for
$\theta_k$:
every distinct tuple has the same expectation, while the normalized tuple
weights sum to one. Uniform weights recover Hoeffding's ordinary estimator.
Observation-dependent weights instead define a reweighted target and require
the sampling assumptions discussed in Section~\ref{sec:framework}.

\subsubsection{Fast computation from weighted bivariate ranks}

Direct evaluation of the preceding sums would require up to $O(n^5)$
operations. They can be collapsed to one-dimensional and bivariate rank sums.
For $a=1,\ldots,4$, define

\begin{align*}
  r_i^{(a)}
  &= \sum_{j=1}^n p_j^a I_{j,i}^X,
  &
  s_i^{(a)}
  &= \sum_{j=1}^n p_j^a I_{j,i}^Y,
  &
  q_i^{(a)}
  &= \sum_{j=1}^n p_j^a I_{j,i}^{XY}.
\end{align*}

Thus $r_i^{(1)}$ and $s_i^{(1)}$ are strict weighted marginal ranks, while
$q_i^{(a)}$ is a weighted bivariate rank computed with the $a$th powers of
the case masses.

Algorithm~\ref{alg:weighted-bivariate-ranks} computes all bivariate ranks for
one set of input masses. It is a merge sort over records initially ordered by
$X$. Each recursive call returns its records ordered by $Y$. When a record
from the right half is emitted, the accumulated mass of smaller-$Y$ records
from the left half is added to its rank. Those records also precede it in
$X$, so the update counts exactly the newly exposed lower-left quadrant. The
strict comparison in line~\ref{alg:dominance-strict} makes equal $Y$ values
noncontributing, although the present Hoeffding estimator assumes no marginal
ties.

\begin{algorithm}[ht]
  \caption{Weighted bivariate ranks by dominance merge sort}
  \label{alg:weighted-bivariate-ranks}
  \small
  \begin{algorithmic}[1]
    \Procedure{WeightedBivariateRanks}{$(X_i,Y_i,u_i)_{i=1}^n$}
      \State Sort the records by increasing $X_i$
      \State $\mathcal Z\gets$ the sorted record sequence
      \State Set $q_i\gets0$ for every record
      \State $\mathcal Z\gets\Call{DominanceMerge}{\mathcal Z}$
      \State \Return $(q_i)_{i=1}^n$ in the original observation order
    \EndProcedure
    \Statex
    \Function{DominanceMerge}{$\mathcal Z$}
      \If{$|\mathcal Z|\leq1$}
        \State \Return $\mathcal Z$
      \EndIf
      \State Split $\mathcal Z$ into consecutive $X$-order halves
        $\mathcal Z_L,\mathcal Z_R$
      \State $\mathcal L\gets\Call{DominanceMerge}{\mathcal Z_L}$
        \Comment{sorted by $Y$}
      \State $\mathcal R\gets\Call{DominanceMerge}{\mathcal Z_R}$
        \Comment{sorted by $Y$}
      \State $P_{\mathrm{passed}}\gets0$, $\mathcal M\gets$ empty sequence
      \While{$\mathcal L$ and $\mathcal R$ are both nonempty}
        \If{$Y_{\mathcal L_1}<Y_{\mathcal R_1}$}
          \label{alg:dominance-strict}
          \State Append $\mathcal L_1$ to $\mathcal M$,
            $P_{\mathrm{passed}}\gets P_{\mathrm{passed}}+u_{\mathcal L_1}$,
            and remove $\mathcal L_1$
        \Else
          \State $q_{\mathcal R_1}\gets q_{\mathcal R_1}+P_{\mathrm{passed}}$
          \State Append $\mathcal R_1$ to $\mathcal M$ and remove
            $\mathcal R_1$
        \EndIf
      \EndWhile
      \ForAll{records remaining in $\mathcal R$, in order}
        \State $q_{\mathcal R_1}\gets q_{\mathcal R_1}+P_{\mathrm{passed}}$
        \State Append $\mathcal R_1$ to $\mathcal M$ and remove
          $\mathcal R_1$
      \EndFor
      \State Append the records remaining in $\mathcal L$ to $\mathcal M$
      \State \Return $\mathcal M$
    \EndFunction
  \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{proposition}[Rank representation]
\label{prop:weighted-hoeffding-ranks}
Let at least five case masses be positive. Define

\begin{align*}
  A_1
  &= \sum_{i=1}^n p_i
     \left\{\left(q_i^{(1)}\right)^2-q_i^{(2)}\right\},\\
  A_2
  &= \sum_{i=1}^n p_i
     \left\{
       r_i^{(1)}s_i^{(1)}q_i^{(1)}
       -q_i^{(2)}\left(r_i^{(1)}+s_i^{(1)}+q_i^{(1)}\right)
       +2q_i^{(3)}
     \right\},\\
  A_3
  &= \sum_{i=1}^n p_i
     \Bigl[
       \left\{\left(r_i^{(1)}\right)^2-r_i^{(2)}\right\}
       \left\{\left(s_i^{(1)}\right)^2-s_i^{(2)}\right\}\\
  &\qquad
       -4\left\{
         \left(r_i^{(1)}s_i^{(1)}-q_i^{(2)}\right)q_i^{(2)}
         -q_i^{(3)}\left(r_i^{(1)}+s_i^{(1)}\right)
         +2q_i^{(4)}
       \right\}\\
  &\qquad
       -2\left\{\left(q_i^{(2)}\right)^2-q_i^{(4)}\right\}
     \Bigr].
\end{align*}

Then the distinct-tuple estimator satisfies

\begin{align*}
  \widehat D_w
  &= 30\left\{
       \frac{A_1}{3!e_3(p_1,\ldots,p_n)}
       -\frac{2A_2}{4!e_4(p_1,\ldots,p_n)}
       +\frac{A_3}{5!e_5(p_1,\ldots,p_n)}
     \right\}.
\end{align*}
\end{proposition}

The powers of the masses are diagonal corrections, not an additional choice
of weighting scheme. For example,

\begin{align*}
  \left(q_i^{(1)}\right)^2-q_i^{(2)}
  &= \sum_{j\neq k}p_jp_k I_{j,i}^{XY}I_{k,i}^{XY},
\end{align*}

which gives $A_1$ after multiplication by the anchor mass $p_i$. For $A_2$,
the unrestricted product $q_i^{(1)}r_i^{(1)}s_i^{(1)}$ allows its three
comparison indices to coincide. Removing the three pairwise diagonals and
restoring the common triple diagonal gives

\begin{align*}
  q_i^{(1)}r_i^{(1)}s_i^{(1)}
  -q_i^{(2)}\left(q_i^{(1)}+r_i^{(1)}+s_i^{(1)}\right)
  +2q_i^{(3)}.
\end{align*}

For $A_3$, the first product in braces makes the two $X$ indices distinct and
the two $Y$ indices distinct. Four possible cross-collisions remain. The term
multiplied by four is the mass of configurations with one specified
cross-collision and two remaining distinct indices; the final term, multiplied
by two, removes the two possible matchings with two cross-collisions. This
partitions all repeated-index configurations and proves the rank
representation. A fully indexed version of the argument is given in
Appendix~\ref{app:hoeffding-identities}.

Algorithm~\ref{alg:weighted-hoeffding} assembles the complete calculation.
The marginal calls use the strict-rank output of
Algorithm~\ref{alg:weighted-ranks}; normalization of the supplied masses is
unnecessary because each homogeneous term is divided by a normalizer of the
same degree.

\begin{algorithm}[ht]
  \caption{Fast weighted Hoeffding estimator}
  \label{alg:weighted-hoeffding}
  \small
  \begin{algorithmic}[1]
    \Procedure{WeightedHoeffding}{$(X_i,Y_i,p_i)_{i=1}^n$}
      \State Compute $r^{(1)}$ and $s^{(1)}$ by weighted strict-rank scans
        with masses $p_i$
      \State Compute $r^{(2)}$ and $s^{(2)}$ by weighted strict-rank scans
        with masses $p_i^2$
      \For{$a=1,\ldots,4$}
        \State $q^{(a)}\gets
          \Call{WeightedBivariateRanks}{(X_i,Y_i,p_i^a)_{i=1}^n}$
      \EndFor
      \State Compute $e_3(p_1,\ldots,p_n)$, $e_4(p_1,\ldots,p_n)$, and
        $e_5(p_1,\ldots,p_n)$ by Newton's identities
      \State Accumulate $A_1,A_2,A_3$ in one scan using
        Proposition~\ref{prop:weighted-hoeffding-ranks}
      \State \Return
        $30\left\{A_1/(3!e_3)-2A_2/(4!e_4)+A_3/(5!e_5)\right\}$
    \EndProcedure
  \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The marginal ranks $r_i^{(a)}$ and $s_i^{(a)}$ are obtained by sorting and
forming prefix sums. The bivariate ranks are two-dimensional dominance sums.
After sorting observations by $X$, a merge sort of the corresponding $Y$
values accumulates, for every observation, the mass $p_j^a$ of preceding
observations with smaller $Y$. This is weighted inversion counting, analogous
to the merge-sort calculation of Kendall's coefficient. It produces all
$q_i^{(a)}$ for a fixed $a$ in $O(n\log n)$ time and $O(n)$ memory. Four
passes, for $a=1,\ldots,4$, retain the same asymptotic complexity. Classical
fast rank implementations likewise compute unweighted Hoeffding statistics in
$O(n\log n)$ time \citep{evenzohar2020}.

The merge-sort idea itself is classical. The distinctive weighted step is the
combination of four dominance passes with masses $p_i,p_i^2,p_i^3,p_i^4$ and
the elementary-symmetric normalizers. A single dominance pass with $p_i$
would leave repeated-index contributions in the order-three through
order-five products. The higher mass powers remove precisely those diagonals,
as established above and in Appendix~\ref{app:hoeffding-identities}.

Finally, the normalizing terms are evaluated from Newton's identities. Set
$e_0=1$. Then

\begin{align*}
  e_k(p_1,\ldots,p_n)
  &= \frac{1}{k}\sum_{a=1}^k(-1)^{a-1}
     e_{k-a}(p_1,\ldots,p_n)\sum_{i=1}^n p_i^a.
\end{align*}

Only $k=3,4,5$ is needed, so this step is linear in $n$.

\subsubsection{Correctness and computational complexity}

Proposition~\ref{prop:weighted-hoeffding-ranks} shows algebraic equality with
the direct distinct-tuple estimator. The resulting algorithm consists of a
fixed number of sorts, weighted merge passes, and linear scans. Its overall
running time is therefore $O(n\log n)$ and its auxiliary memory requirement is
$O(n)$. Computing each bivariate rank directly would take $O(n^2)$ time, even
after the order-five tuple sums had been reduced algebraically.

The representation also makes several finite-sample properties immediate.
It is invariant under a common positive scaling of the original weights,
under permutations of the observations, and under strictly increasing
transformations of either margin. Exchanging $X$ and $Y$ leaves the statistic
unchanged. Zero-mass observations can be removed before computation. For
uniform weights, the denominators become

\begin{align*}
  k!e_k(1/n,\ldots,1/n)
  &= \frac{n(n-1)\cdots(n-k+1)}{n^k},
\end{align*}

and the rank formula reduces to the classical unweighted statistic.

The current method assumes continuous marginals and hence no ties. Strict and
weak inequalities agree almost surely in that setting. A tie-corrected
weighted Hoeffding coefficient would need a deliberate fractional convention
for bivariate ranks and corresponding diagonal identities; silently applying
midranks would not preserve Proposition~\ref{prop:weighted-hoeffding-ranks}.
Moreover, highly concentrated masses make $e_5(p_1,\ldots,p_n)$ small and the
estimator unstable even when it is formally defined. The asymptotic test in
Section~\ref{sec:independence-tests} therefore requires diffuse weights in
addition to five positive observations.
